## Research priorities

- Pursue results uniform in n. The central goals are O-marked moduli, point schemes, a morphism to moduli of Poisson varieties, and comparison with the Kuznetsov components of the same underlying varieties; equivalence is a research expectation, not an established result on all components.
- Prioritize general or very general objects of each irreducible component. Distinguish dense Zariski opens, geometric generic points, and complements of countably many proper closed subsets. Do not identify a one-block presentation component with all components of the intrinsic moduli.
- Treat the degree-one shift and presentation as auxiliary unless recovered from (C,O). On the one-block dense open, the pinned MF/window/generic-generation/coherent-saturation inputs give the residual cubic, the projective Poisson morphism, and a finite étale comparison to Ku of the same cubic. The point elliptic curve and residual active cubic have equal j; their plane-Isom cover has rank 18, with a rank-9 3-torsion cover of the rank-2 Jacobian-Isom torsor. The scale line is dual to the active Hodge line. The point differential defines a canonical dominant generically finite residual morphism modulo sign and a full lift on that rank-2 cover. Its generic degree is exactly 18: finite mixed-weight triangularity gives a polynomial inverse, and the common Hesse stabilizers give finite étale factors 2,1,9. The generic sign class descends uniquely to the two-variable primitive active-invariant field, with χ_op=χ_sgn+χ_act. Computing χ_act and choosing a canonical sign on the original stack remain open. Distinguish this intermediate cover from the plane-Isom cover; the proof uses geometric generic integrality, not a direct transfer of the old Hessian correction degree. Do not identify the transported point translation with Ku rotation.
- Comparison kernels still require first selecting compatible isomorphism classes under the full autoequivalence action, then descending the scalar gerbe. On a split chart there is a nonempty refined type in which R_P=q_YΦ_P(κ_nc) has H^{>−1}=0 and degree-zero invertible H^{−1}. After first-stage full-kernel class selection, this canonical top cohomology produces a weight-one rank-3 bundle and a degree-3 Azumaya certificate, hence index divides 3. If this type holds after splitting the presentation, the field bound is 3 for n≢1 mod3 and 9 for n≡1 mod3. The older CF Euler type alone retains the bound 3^floor((n+1)/2); do not extend the refined bound to all kernels in that older type. Preserve the first-stage class-selection condition. Do not identify the kernel gerbe with the presentation Brauer class. The chosen strict relative completed bar flow and its inverse descend to homogeneous center quotients, including U=0. Coherent graded-center linearity of a finite algebraic generic kernel remains unproved. Do not equate coefficient quotients with categorical derived tensor or absolute perfectness with relative perfectness. For a preselected kernel class the scalar index is unchanged by prime-to-3 field extensions; the existing rotation Euler lattice is exactly d_n Z. The refined bound uses the full kernel and its canonical top cohomology, without an algebraic active-kernel assignment. In the same split setting, the gerbe equals the pullback of the active curve Picard gerbe along the canonical top-line class m_P; m_P=0 kills the scalar obstruction. It splits on the active curve, and a transverse K-line section gives a rank-3 finite étale scalar splitting cover. These are all conditional on first-stage full-class selection. Apply the negative-rotation sheaf calculation over a field, not to a degenerating Picard family. Keep the distinction between a finite comparison square, its isomorphism-class selection, and coherent center linearity.
- For a selected full kernel class in the refined type, use the determinant construction of a line representing multiplication by 3 on the active Jacobian. The canonical third-division torsor has finite étale rank 9 and carries the full kernel uniquely after its top line is identified with the constructed line. Its scalar obstruction factors through J(S)/3J(S). This is relative scalar descent after class selection, not a new index bound or an intrinsic class-selection theorem.
- The Jacobian-Isom torsor constructed on the explicit C3-symmetric test family splits, and restriction from the Hesse invariant field to C(p,q) is injective on quadratic classes. Identifying that torsor with a specialization of the generic active sign class, global vanishing, and the semiclassically normalized degree-9 full residual map are conditional on E_sigma: a normal neighborhood of that test family carrying a base-change-compatible residual curve.
